# Mode: Academic Write

Drafting scholarly manuscripts, proposals, and theses from locked evidence.

## Objective

Draft clear, rigorous academic prose following standard scholarly conventions (e.g. IMRaD: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion) with explicit citations tied to the claim matrix.

## Procedure

1. **Pre-flight Check:**
   - Confirm verified claim-source matrix exists before drafting.
   - Confirm target venue or structure (e.g. Conference paper, Thesis chapter, Grant proposal, Systematic literature review).

2. **Structural Sections:**
   - **Introduction:** Research problem, real-world/theoretical motivation, gap in existing literature, explicit research questions, contribution summary.
   - **Related Work:** Synthesis by theme or taxonomy, not merely an unorganized serial list. Clearly position the current work relative to priors.
   - **Methodology / System Model:** Formal problem definition, assumptions, mathematical formulation, dataset description, operational procedures, threats to validity.
   - **Evaluation / Results:** Empirical data, statistical significance, comparative baseline analysis, ablation studies.
   - **Discussion & Limitations:** Honest assessment of scope, edge cases, failure modes, ethical considerations, and future work.
   - **Conclusion:** Summary of contributions and final takeaways.

3. **Citation Discipline:**
   - Use standard citation keys (e.g. `[@author2025]` or numerical `[1]`).
   - Every factual premise or comparative claim must carry a citation.
   - Never fabricate author names, publication years, or volume/issue numbers.

4. **Tone & Style:**
   - Precise, objective, third-person or collective first-person ("we observe").
   - Eliminate hyperbole and colloquialisms.
   - Reserve humanizer pass as an optional final step only after academic structure and citations are locked.
