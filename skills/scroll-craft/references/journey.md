# Journey and feeling curve

A beat is a change in what the visitor knows, feels, or decides. Sections serve
beats. Cut a section that serves none.

Example progression, not a required order:

`Recognition → Tension → Discovery → Proof → Emotional Peak → Commitment`

Beat count follows the story, not a section quota. Do not lengthen the page to
chase an effect.

## Table

| Beat | Known/felt before | Change after | Proof content | Device | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Write the **feeling curve** before the device table. One line per beat: the
emotion, then what on screen causes it. Adjacent beats with the same feeling
means one is filler.

Useful states (open list): curiosity, recognition, unease, tension, clarity,
delight, relief, confidence, resolve, calm.

## Peak

Engineer **one** peak. Name it as the sentence a visitor would tell a friend.
The peak gets contrast and the asset budget. It does not have to be the longest
section. Three peaks is none.

The close must resolve and hold. A fade into a leftover footer overwrites the
peak.

## Signature seed

The signature interaction belongs to the domain and teaches the product. Example
shape (synthetic demo, labelled as such): a message appears → indicators
unravel → URL/account/pattern links show → an evidence map forms. Do not claim
real backend detection.

Generic fade/parallax/scale is not a signature because it got a new name. The
signature keeps a static, keyboard, and reduced-motion equivalent so meaning
survives.

## Interview

Do not run an eight-question interview when the brief already answers. Use user
instructions and assets. Mark assumptions. Do not invent quotes, brand voice,
product claims, or interview answers.
